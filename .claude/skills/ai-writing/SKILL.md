# AI Writing: Human-Voice Prose

Adapted from [ContractorKeith/ai-writing-skill](https://github.com/ContractorKeith/ai-writing-skill)
for this repo. The original targets long-form blog content in one author's personal voice;
this version keeps only the general anti-AI-tell rules and drops the persona/voice-anchor
material, since it doesn't apply to CVs, cover letters, or LinkedIn posts written for Keenan.

Read `references/ai-writing-antipatterns.md` for the full catalogue with examples when a
deeper check is warranted; the rules below are the working summary.

## Writing rules

### Voice and tone
- First person or direct second person. A point of view, not a wall of description.
- Vary sentence length on purpose. At least one sentence under 8 words and one over 25
  words in anything longer than a couple of paragraphs.
- Use sentence fragments occasionally. Not every sentence needs a subject and verb.
- Let some sentences start with "And," "But," "So," or "Because." AI text almost never does.

### Banned words and phrases
Avoid these in their figurative/AI-typical sense (fine if quoting someone or using the
literal meaning):

delve, foster/fostering, bolster, garner, showcase, tapestry, vibrant (abstract), meticulous,
intricate, interplay, underscore (verb), pivotal, enduring, enhance, landscape (abstract),
leverage (verb), navigate (figurative), robust (non-physical), comprehensive, align with,
valuable insights, stands as/serves as (when "is" works), is a testament to, vital/crucial/key
role, evolving landscape, nestled, in the heart of, renowned, groundbreaking, boasts a,
commitment to, "It's important/worth noting," "In today's [anything]," "When it comes to,"
"Whether you're a [X] or a [Y]," "Not just X, but also Y."

Prefer plain copulas: "is," "are," "was," "has." Don't dress them up as "serves as" or
"represents" when the simple verb works.

### Structure
- No "Challenges and Future Outlook"-style closing sections.
- No dictionary-entry openings that define the topic before saying anything.
- No rule-of-three lists everywhere ("adjective, adjective, and adjective") — vary list
  length.
- No trailing "...highlighting the importance of X" / "...underscoring Y" participle
  endings. State the point directly.
- No vague attribution ("experts say," "many believe") — name the source or drop the claim.

### Formatting
- No em dashes. Use commas, parentheses, colons, or periods instead.
- Sentence-case headings, not Title Case.
- No emoji as decoration.
- Bold sparingly — for genuine emphasis, not as a substitute for a clearer sentence.
- Tables only when actually comparing 3+ items across 2+ dimensions.

### Content quality
- Lead with something specific — a number, a place, a date — before any general claim.
- Include concrete, checkable detail rather than vague enthusiasm.
- Let the point stand without appending "This shows..." after it.
- Calibrate confidence honestly; hedge when the situation is actually uncertain.

## Self-check before delivering

1. Any banned words used figuratively? Rewrite those sentences.
2. Any em dashes? Replace all of them.
3. Any trailing "-ing" participle summaries? Rewrite as a direct claim.
4. Headings in Title Case? Convert to sentence case.
5. Does it open by defining the topic instead of just saying the thing? Restructure.
6. Does it end with a formulaic "In conclusion..." wrap-up? Cut it.
7. Is there at least one concrete, specific detail (name, number, date, place)?
